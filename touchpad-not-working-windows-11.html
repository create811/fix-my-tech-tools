Touchpad Not Working in Windows 11

A touchpad that suddenly stops working can feel dramatic because it takes away your main way to control the laptop. The good news is that touchpad failures are often caused by settings, function-key toggles, external mouse behavior, or driver changes rather than an instantly dead touchpad.

Quick answer

Check the touchpad toggle and disconnect other mice first

Quick answer

Start by checking the laptop's Fn touchpad key, disconnecting any USB or Bluetooth mouse, and confirming the touchpad is enabled in Windows settings before moving into drivers or BIOS.

Safe order

Step-by-step order for touchpad problems

  1. Use an external mouse if possible. This makes the rest of the checks much easier.
  2. Check the Fn touchpad toggle. Many laptops can disable the touchpad with one key combination.
  3. Disconnect Bluetooth and USB mice. Some systems change touchpad behavior when another pointer device is connected.
  4. Review Windows touchpad settings. Make sure the touchpad is enabled and not hidden by a setting.
  5. Check Device Manager. Look for touchpad, HID, or I2C devices that are missing or flagged.
  6. Check Windows Update and the laptop maker's driver page. This matters especially if the problem began after an update.
  7. Check BIOS or UEFI if the touchpad is completely absent. Firmware settings can disable it below Windows.
  8. Consider physical damage. Bulging, lifting, or erratic clicking can point to hardware pressure or battery swelling.

Symptom guide

What the touchpad symptom usually means

If the cursor does not move at all, start with the toggle key and device detection. If the touchpad clicks but movement is erratic, drivers or physical pressure can be involved. If the touchpad vanished from settings, Windows may not be loading the correct device driver. If it stopped right after an update, the driver path is more likely than a sudden hardware failure.

Settings and external devices

Why the simple checks matter more than people expect

Touchpads are easy to disable by accident. Many laptops have a dedicated symbol on a function key for this, and users often trigger it without noticing while adjusting brightness or volume. External mice also complicate things. Some systems reduce or disable touchpad behavior automatically when another pointing device is attached.

That is why disconnecting a Bluetooth or USB mouse is not a throwaway step. It tells you whether the laptop is treating the touchpad as secondary rather than broken.

Device detection

Check Device Manager for HID and I2C clues

In Device Manager, a modern precision touchpad may appear under Human Interface Devices, Mice and other pointing devices, or as an I2C-related device tied to the chipset. If the touchpad is missing or has a warning icon, the driver path becomes more important.

The How To Update Drivers Windows 11 guide is the safe path when you need to refresh the touchpad or chipset driver without letting random updater software make decisions for you.

After updates

When a Windows update is part of the story

Touchpads sometimes stop behaving normally after a Windows update because the precision touchpad driver, chipset layer, or laptop vendor package changed in the background. If that timing matches what happened on your machine, the Windows 11 Running Slow After Update guide can help you confirm whether Windows is still settling down or whether the driver change likely needs attention.

Firmware and hardware

BIOS settings and physical issues come later, but they matter

If the touchpad is completely absent in Windows after normal driver checks, BIOS or UEFI may be disabling it. This is more likely after a firmware reset, recent repair, or a board-level issue. Hardware is also worth suspecting when the touchpad clicks strangely, the palm rest is lifting, or the chassis looks uneven.

Common mistakes

What not to do with a dead touchpad

  • Assuming the touchpad failed physically before checking the Fn toggle
  • Leaving a Bluetooth or USB mouse connected during every test
  • Updating multiple unrelated drivers at once without a reason
  • Ignoring BIOS or UEFI when the touchpad is completely absent from Windows
  • Continuing to press on a lifting or bulging touchpad area

Hardware warning

Stop using the laptop if the touchpad area is lifting or bulging

A touchpad that feels raised, stiff, or difficult to click can be a battery swelling warning rather than just a pointer problem. If the chassis or touchpad area is lifting or bulging, stop using the laptop until the battery is checked. The Signs Laptop Battery Needs Replacement guide covers the warning signs in more detail.

FAQ

Why did my touchpad stop working in Windows 11?

A touchpad can stop working because it was disabled with a function key, turned off in Windows settings, replaced by an external mouse setting, affected by a bad driver, or disabled in BIOS or UEFI.

What if the touchpad is missing from Windows settings?

If the touchpad section is missing from settings, Windows may not be detecting the device correctly. Check Device Manager for HID or I2C touchpad devices and review the laptop maker's touchpad driver.

Can an external mouse make the touchpad seem disabled?

Yes. Some laptops disable the touchpad automatically when a USB or Bluetooth mouse is connected, so disconnecting the external mouse is a worthwhile early test.

Can a Windows update break the touchpad?

Yes. A Windows update can change a precision touchpad, HID, or chipset-related driver, which is why touchpad issues sometimes start immediately after updates.

When should I check BIOS or UEFI for the touchpad?

Check BIOS or UEFI after basic Windows settings and driver checks if the touchpad is completely absent or stayed disabled after a reset, firmware update, or repair.

Can battery swelling affect a touchpad?

Yes. A swollen battery can push up on the chassis and make the touchpad click oddly, stick, or stop responding. If the palm rest or touchpad area is lifting or bulging, stop using the laptop and inspect the battery immediately.
