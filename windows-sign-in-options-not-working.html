Windows sign-in troubleshooting

Windows Sign-In Options Not Working? Password, PIN and Windows Hello Fixes

When Windows sign-in options are missing, greyed out, or simply stop working, the cause is often broader than one broken PIN. The problem may involve Windows Hello, account state, fingerprint or camera hardware, drivers, or policy restrictions on a managed device. The key is to separate a single-method failure from a multi-method sign-in problem.

Quick answer

Figure out which sign-in methods are affected before changing anything

Quick answer

If Windows sign-in options are not working, first identify whether the issue affects password, PIN, fingerprint, face recognition, or several methods at once. That tells you whether you are dealing with Windows Hello, hardware, account, or policy trouble.

Safe order

Best troubleshooting order for broken or missing sign-in options

  1. Check which methods are affected. Password, PIN, fingerprint, and face sign-in do not fail for the same reasons.
  2. Open Sign-in options and review what is available.
  3. Confirm whether the device hardware supports fingerprint or face sign-in.
  4. Check whether recent updates, password changes, or account changes happened first.
  5. Review drivers for the camera, fingerprint reader, and related hardware.
  6. Consider work or school policy restrictions on managed devices.
  7. Use the password or another working method to stay in control while troubleshooting.

Intent separation

This page is broader than a PIN-only failure

If the main issue is that the PIN is specifically rejected, unavailable, or forgotten, use Windows 11 PIN Not Working. This page is for the broader situation where multiple sign-in methods are missing, unavailable, or acting strangely together.

That includes cases where fingerprint sign-in disappeared, face recognition stopped working, and the Sign-in options area no longer looks the way it used to.

Hardware side

Fingerprint and face sign-in depend on real hardware and working drivers

Windows Hello features are not just software toggles. Face sign-in depends on compatible camera hardware. Fingerprint sign-in depends on a working fingerprint reader and driver. If those devices are missing, disabled, or unstable, Windows may hide the related sign-in options or make them unreliable.

If the face-recognition path broke after the webcam changed behavior, check Webcam Not Working in Windows 11 as a companion guide.

Accounts and policy

Local accounts, Microsoft accounts, and managed policies change the diagnosis

A home laptop using a local account is a different situation from a work-issued device signed into Microsoft or Entra-managed services. On managed PCs, policy can control which sign-in methods are allowed. If settings look unavailable rather than merely broken, policy is a strong suspect.

On those systems, administrator involvement is often the cleanest path rather than trying to force the options back manually.

Updates and drivers

Windows updates and device drivers can quietly remove or destabilize sign-in methods

Recent updates can change camera, fingerprint, security, and chipset behavior. If Windows Hello stopped working after a patch cycle, compare the timing with your update history. The safe driver walkthrough is How To Update Drivers Windows 11.

If the entire login flow hangs rather than just hiding options, the better fit is Windows 11 Stuck on Login Screen.

When to escalate

When organization policy or deeper system issues are more likely

  • Several sign-in methods disappear at once after a managed-device change
  • The fingerprint reader or camera is missing in Device Manager
  • The Sign-in options page shows settings as unavailable due to policy
  • The password works but every Windows Hello option fails together
  • The device belongs to work or school and security settings are centrally controlled

FAQ

Why are Windows sign-in options missing or not working?

Windows sign-in options can disappear or fail because of Windows Hello issues, device driver problems, missing hardware support, account changes, recent updates, or work and school policies.

What is the difference between this problem and a PIN-only problem?

A PIN-only problem affects the PIN specifically, while this broader issue involves multiple sign-in methods such as password, PIN, fingerprint, or face recognition being unavailable or malfunctioning.

Can fingerprint or face sign-in fail because of hardware support?

Yes. Fingerprint and face sign-in depend on compatible hardware plus the correct drivers, so missing or faulty camera and fingerprint hardware can remove those options.

Can work or school policy remove sign-in options?

Yes. On managed devices, administrators can restrict or disable Windows Hello methods through policy.

Should I check updates and drivers for sign-in-option problems?

Yes. Sign-in options can break after updates or when fingerprint, camera, chipset, or security-related drivers are not working correctly.

When should I involve an administrator?

Involve an administrator when the device is work-managed, policy appears to control the setting, or several sign-in methods are failing in a way that normal account troubleshooting cannot explain.
