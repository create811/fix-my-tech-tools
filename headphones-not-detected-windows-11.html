Headphones Not Detected in Windows 11? Step-by-Step Fix

When wired or USB headphones are physically connected but Windows 11 acts like they are not there, the problem is often detection rather than sound quality. That distinction matters, because the best starting steps are the port, plug, output list, and jack-detection path, not generic Bluetooth advice or random sound tweaks.

Quick answer

Start with the connection type and output list

Quick answer

If headphones are not detected in Windows 11, first confirm whether they are 3.5 mm or USB, reconnect them firmly, try another port, and check the output device list before moving into drivers or Realtek settings.

Safe order

Best troubleshooting order for undetected headphones

  1. Identify the connection type. A 3.5 mm headset and a USB headset fail in different ways.
  2. Reconnect the headphones fully. Half-seated plugs are more common than people think.
  3. Try another port if possible. That matters for both USB headsets and audio jacks on docks.
  4. Check the correct output device. Windows may still be using speakers, monitor audio, or a different headset.
  5. Review jack-detection or manufacturer audio software. Realtek-style prompts can silently choose the wrong device type.
  6. Check Device Manager and the driver.
  7. Test the headphones on another device. This tells you whether the headset itself is the real problem.

3.5 mm versus USB

Why wired jack headphones and USB headsets should not be treated the same way

A 3.5 mm headphone plug depends on the laptop's analog audio jack and its detection software. A USB headset behaves more like its own external audio device. That means USB adds the port, cable, dock, and USB power path into the diagnosis, while a 3.5 mm headset leans harder on jack fit, combo-port compatibility, and the laptop's audio package.

If your issue is broader and Windows has no sound anywhere, use No Sound in Windows 11. This page is for the narrower case where the headphones themselves are the device Windows is failing to notice properly.

Combo jack check

Combo headset jacks can confuse older accessories and ports

Many modern laptops use one combo audio jack for both headphones and microphone. Older desktops and some accessories still assume separate green and pink ports. If you plug a combo headset into a system expecting separate connections, Windows may partially detect it or not route the audio the way you expect.

If your headset includes both headphones and microphone and the input side is the part failing, the follow-up guide is Microphone Not Working in Windows 11.

Realtek and vendor software

Jack-detection pop-ups and manufacturer audio software can be the hidden cause

On many laptops, Realtek or a manufacturer utility asks whether the newly connected device is headphones, a headset, speakers, or line-in. If that prompt was dismissed, misread, or broken by an update, the headphones may be physically fine while Windows routes them incorrectly or never exposes them cleanly in the output list.

This is one reason the laptop maker's audio package can matter more than a generic driver. If detection broke right after updates, compare Windows Update with the support page for your exact model.

USB headset angle

USB headphones need basic USB troubleshooting too

If the headphones are USB, plug them directly into the laptop or desktop instead of through a hub or dock. If Windows does not react at all, or the headset appears and disappears, the USB path may be the problem rather than audio settings. In that case, use USB Device Not Recognized in Windows alongside this guide.

If the headset is detected but connects over Bluetooth and still has no sound, that is a different intent covered in Bluetooth Headphones Connected but No Sound.

Hardware signs

When the headset or headphone jack is more likely physically at fault

  • The plug only works when pushed or twisted a certain way
  • The jack feels loose or visibly damaged
  • The headset fails on another known-good device
  • USB headset detection drops in and out with slight cable movement
  • Several known-good headsets fail in the same laptop port

Once you see those signs, keep repeated plug cycles to a minimum and stop assuming a driver reinstall will solve a physical connection fault.

FAQ

Why are my headphones not detected in Windows 11?

Windows 11 may not detect headphones because of a loose jack or USB connection, the wrong output device, driver trouble, jack-detection software, a combo-port mismatch, or physical damage to the headset or port.

What is the difference between a combo audio jack and separate ports?

A combo jack is designed for a headset plug that carries both audio and microphone. Older separate headphone and microphone ports may not detect a combo headset correctly without the right adapter.

Can Realtek audio software affect headphone detection?

Yes. Realtek or manufacturer audio software can control jack pop-ups, device-type prompts, and detection behavior, so headphone problems sometimes come from the vendor audio package rather than the headset itself.

Should I test the headphones on another device?

Yes. Testing the same headphones on a phone, another PC, or another laptop quickly tells you whether the headset itself is faulty.

What if USB headphones are not detected?

USB headphones add the USB port, cable, dock, and power path to the problem. Try another direct USB port and use the USB device troubleshooting guide if Windows does not see the headset consistently.

When is the headphone jack more likely damaged?

The jack is more likely damaged if detection works only when the plug is held a certain way, the port feels loose, audio crackles, or several known-good headsets fail in the same port.
